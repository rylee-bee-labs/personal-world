## Your DESIGN.md

# v4065 — Annotated plates (MOCKUP)

## 1. Metaphor in one line, and the main gesture.
Worlds is a studio wall of hand-drawn plates: each service is a small ink drawing of its own object (a shelf, a door), and every fact is a numbered callout on that drawing. The main gesture is **tap a callout to read its source, freshness, who can see and who can act**. The drawing is the fact card; the pencil line is the provenance.

## 2. Integration model: how a service plugs in, where it lives in the shell, how its facts flow.
Start: `media-shelf` (Healthy) and `front-door` (Needs a human), fixture ids, names, states and facts verbatim. `#add-service` opens Connect for `notes-sync` (Unavailable): (1) Discover, (2) Credential reference (reference only; a pasted secret is refused with "Worlds stores a credential reference, never the secret"), (3) Safe test (run / running / passed / failed + recovery), (4) Preview `sync.provider-reachable` (unavailable), `sync.local-pending` (3 notes, live), `sync.last-synced` (stale), (5) Choose what to publish (one checkbox per fact), (6) Publish. Step buttons are real `<button>`s. Mock only; no provider call.
One in-memory store keyed by fact id feeds Home, the plate, and Agent facts. A 4s live tick (ON by default) moves freshness ("checked 2s ago" → "12s ago") and now and then marks a fact stale, and all three views update with no reload. `#simulate-change` applies `lock-back-door` to `frontdoor.back-door`: the fact becomes `locked`, Front Door becomes healthy, attention count 0, in all three views at once (the Worlds-side result is a mock review only; nothing is locked). Optimistic: the callout flips at once, then settles with a visible "recorded (mock)" line.

## 3. Navigation between Worlds and a service (phone and desktop).
Persistent shell destinations: Home, Connect, Memory, Settings. Desktop: left rail, plate register in the middle, selected plate and docked app on the right. Phone: bottom tab bar with the same four; a selected plate is a full screen with explicit Back, focus moves to its heading and returns to the invoking row. First screen has Front Door selected, so `#disconnect` is visible without opening anything; it asks "Disconnect Front Door?" with a button labelled "Disconnect" and a Cancel.

## 4. The embedded-app frame (sandbox, fallback, what the shell keeps around it).
The owning app sits in a plain inset frame beside (desktop) or below (phone) the plate, titled with owner and state. Fallback is always visible at its edge: Media Shelf "Open Media app" `app://media`, Front Door "Open Home app" `app://home`, Notes Sync "Open Memory app" `app://memory`. The frame is given no Worlds credential by intent; real enforcement and sandbox flags are UNKNOWN. If embedding fails, the shell, tabs, plate and callouts stay, with a plain explanation and the owner link. Worlds keeps nav, Back, status strip and live toggle around it.

## 5. The agent/human shared-facts model (source, freshness, who sees, who acts).
Agent facts is a table over the same records. Columns: fact, source (`local`/`external`/`none`), observed time and freshness word, Agent can see, Human can see, Who can act. `frontdoor.back-door`: agent read, human act ("Locking the door changes the external system and is the person's decision."). `frontdoor.guest-code-used`: agent `none` as a named, focusable, disabled control with the verbatim fixture reason ("Identifying a person is human-only in this fixture; the agent sees that a guest code was used, not who."). `frontdoor.unlocked-for`: both read. Acting hands off to Project Home (approvals) or the owning app; never decided in Worlds. Notes Sync keeps provider-unavailable and local-pending-live as separate rows. Retry "Check provider now" is an owner handoff.

## 6. States: empty/first run (zero services, zero providers), quiet, needs-you, stale, unknown, disconnected.
- First run: one blank dotted plate, "A plate waiting for its drawing", a Connect invitation, no invented readings.
- Quiet: Media Shelf, Healthy, a calm drawing. Needs-you: Front Door, "Needs a human" with the fixture reason; marked by a word and a drawn mark, not colour alone.
- Stale / unknown: said in words with age; unknown never blank. Unavailable: Notes Sync, local notes still work.
- Error: a failed safe test shows the reason, a retry button and "nothing was saved".
- Disconnected: plate erased with a gentle redraw-to-blank; says "Front Door is gone from Worlds: its facts, plate and frame. Other services are untouched." Retention UNKNOWN.
- Whimsy, small: a tiny drawn pencil sketches the last connector line when a service connects ("Notes Sync is on the wall"); empty-state sparrow doodle; kind micro-copy. Motion is short (≤300ms), data keeps ticking and the motion is removed under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 7. Borrowed from Homarr / Dashy / HA cards / MCP ext-apps, and what is deliberately different.
Homarr: one integration feeding UI and agent view. Dashy: open the app in place. HA cards: literal state words. MCP ext-apps: contained UI under a host boundary. Different: facts are not a widget grid; each is annotated on an object drawing, and the drawing does not route or map between services.

## 8. Your OpenDesign starting point (skill + design system) and how you used it; which guide rules you applied.
Skill `hand-drawn-diagrams`: wobbly monoline SVG strokes, numbered leader-line annotations, a stroke-draw on state change (the skill's animated-SVG idea, static fallback). Its Excalidraw output and hosted link are not used; drawings are inline SVG. System `atelier-zero`: Paper `#efe7d2`, Bone `#f7f1de`, Ink `#15140f`, Ink-mute `#5a5448`, Coral `#ed6f5c` as the single accent, Roman section marks (I. Home, II. Connect…), hairline rules, 9.5–38px scale, 18px card radius, transitions ≤0.25s. Adjustments: no external fonts (system serif italic stands in for Playfair, system sans for Inter Tight); coral is non-text only, button labels in Ink (white on coral fails contrast); drop the paper-noise overlay and the 36px side rails (they cost mobile width); no collage photos. Rules applied: anti-ai-slop, state-coverage, accessibility-baseline (4.5:1 text, 3:1 controls/focus, 44px), animation-discipline, no emoji, no invented metrics.

## 9. Signature element, hero thesis, reference lock.
Signature: the annotated plate, a drawn object with numbered callouts tied to fact records. Thesis: "Every fact is pinned to the thing it came from." Reference lock: Monocle's annotated figure plates and Kurzgesagt-free field-guide labelling (mechanics only: numbered keys and leader lines, no palette).

## 10. Risks and UNKNOWNs.
Embed support, enforced isolation, the Connect API, retention after disconnect: UNKNOWN. Wobbly drawings must stay legible and never replace the text state. Leader lines on a narrow screen collapse into numbered rows. Stale events are simulated, not fixture data. Fixture is synthetic; the page stays labelled MOCKUP.

## 11. How it differs from C, and what it keeps.
Keeps: the real shell (Home / Connect / Memory / Settings), fixed owner-labelled services, the app docked beside a shared fact card, honest state words, the fixture's exact permissions, calm structure. Differs: C's bezel and fact table become a hand-drawn plate whose callouts carry the facts; type and paper come from Atelier Zero rather than skeuomorphism; there is a live feed, warmth and motion where C was static; and mobile has its own navigation and reading order.

## 12. Desktop and mobile layouts.
**Desktop, 1440px:** 220px rail (Roman-numbered destinations, MOCKUP label, `#live-toggle` with "Live, checked 2s ago"); a 320px register of services (name, state word, freshness); the main area shows the selected plate with callouts, the docked app frame with its fallback link, and the fact card; `#disconnect` and `#simulate-change` sit in the plate header and `#add-service` in the register header. Agent facts is a destination tab beside the plate.
**Tablet, 768px:** rail collapses to labelled icons; register above, plate and frame stacked.
**Mobile, 390px:** bottom tab bar (four 44px+ tabs); Home is a single column: attention line, then service rows. Selected plate is a full screen: Back, plate (callouts as numbered markers), numbered fact list (tap for a bottom sheet with source, freshness, who sees, who acts), app frame, fallback link, then `#disconnect` and `#simulate-change`. `#live-toggle` sits in the top bar. No sideways scroll; reading order is status, facts, app, actions.
Checker ids: `#add-service`, `#disconnect` (visible on first screen), `#simulate-change`, `#live-toggle`; MOCKUP label always visible.

## CRITIQUE.md (another engine)

# CRITIQUE — v4065 "Annotated plates"

Reviewed against `concept-brief-v3.md` (which re-applies `concept-brief-v2.md` in full),
`reference/direction-C-DESIGN.md` and `fixture/fixture.json`. Five gaps, most important
first. None of these is a taste preference; each is a requirement the design claims to
meet, or something the checker contract will test.

## 1. The shell advertises four destinations and a first-screen Disconnect; only one destination and one reading of "first screen" are actually designed.

**What is missing/wrong.** §3 and §12 make Home, Connect, Memory and Settings persistent
shell destinations, but no screen is defined for Connect, Memory or Settings. Settings is
named explicitly in the brief's "real rounds" list ("real navigation, a settings screen, a
first-run / empty state, an error state") and in v4065 it exists only as a Roman numeral in
a rail. Desktop "Home" is never distinguished from the "320px register of services" in §12 —
it is unclear whether Home *is* the register or sits beside it. On 390px the landing state is
undefined and both readings fail: §12 makes Home a single-column service list and the plate a
*separate* full screen with Back, while §3 claims "First screen has Front Door selected, so
`#disconnect` is visible without opening anything". If mobile opens on Home-as-list, the
checker requirement — `#disconnect` visible on the first screen "not hidden until something is
opened" — fails; if it opens on the plate, the nav says Home while showing a service screen.

**Why it matters.** The first thing the round adds is "a real application, something she would
use every day". A nav whose destinations have no content is still a concept on a page, and the
checker is an automated script that will load the default view and look for `#disconnect`.

**Concrete fix.** Pin, in DESIGN.md, what each of the four destinations renders at 1440 and at
390, and build Settings from fixture material that the design currently ignores: Local Weather
(`not_configured`, owner fallback "Open provider settings", `app://settings/providers`),
Nightly Backup (`stale`, "Open Backup settings", `app://settings/backups`), the step-5 publish
checkboxes per service, the live-feed interval, and the reduced-motion override. Group it into
labelled sections rather than one flat list (laws-of-ux on settings pages). Then state the 390
landing screen in one sentence and put the *selected* service's `#disconnect` on it.

## 2. The live feed cannot be paused as written, and its one invented transition ignores the fixture's own provenance rule.

**What is wrong.** §2: the tick is ON by default, "moves freshness ('checked 2s ago' → '12s
ago') and now and then marks a fact stale"; §12 exposes `#live-toggle` to pause/resume. But
if the age is derived from the fixture's fixed `checkedAt` (14:23:58Z for `media-shelf`), it
advances from the wall clock and pausing the feed changes nothing; if instead each tick
re-checks, the design is inventing observations. "Now and then" is a random rule, and the
fixture's `_meta.provenanceFields` says a viewer "should recompute age from checkedAt rather
than trusting this number" — staleness has to follow a stated window, not chance. §12's
sample label "Live, checked 2s ago" is also a reading the fixture does not contain.

**Why it matters.** Reactivity is the headline of the round ("a gentle tick … updates every
view … Provide a visible pause/resume"), and `#live-toggle` is a checker id. An unpausable
pause is the one visible failure the whole feed can have, and an invented stale event is
dishonest data dressed as a live reading.

**Concrete fix.** Define a simulated *now* separate from real time: ages = simulated now −
`checkedAt`; the feed is a scripted event list drawn only from fixture records (a re-check,
the Notes Sync provider timing out again, the backup window passing); pausing freezes
simulated now and the control reads "Paused — ages frozen". Make going-stale a stated
per-service window that also changes the state *word*, not just the number. Caption every
simulated change as simulated.

## 3. The one "optimistic" moment is the mock lock — which is also a decision made inside Worlds, contradicting the design's own §5 and integration requirement 5.

**What is wrong.** §2 gives `#simulate-change` the only optimistic treatment: the callout
"flips at once, then settles with a visible 'recorded (mock)' line". But §5 says acting
"hands off to Project Home (approvals) or the owning app; never decided in Worlds", and
requirement 5 is exactly that. The reconciliation ("the Worlds-side result is a mock review
only; nothing is locked") lives in the design document, not in the copy a person sees: a
button in the plate header makes Front Door healthy and the attention count 0 inside Worlds.
Meanwhile requirement 4 asks for optimistic feedback on actions generally, and publish /
unpublish, retry handoff, disconnect and pause have no specified feedback at all.

**Why it matters.** As written, the demo breaks the governance rule it claims to honour, and
the actions a person actually takes have no settle behaviour — so "actions give instant
optimistic feedback and settle visibly" is only half-demonstrated.

**Concrete fix.** Reframe `#simulate-change` as an *inbound* decision ("Project Home approved
the lock; Worlds recorded it (mock)") so the UI never claims Worlds decided, and show the
handoff link alongside it. Then specify one optimistic→settle pattern for every user action:
press feedback ≤100ms, confirmation at ~150ms (animation-discipline), the row/callout flips
immediately, and it settles with a plain "recorded (mock)" or "handed to Project Home" line —
reverting and saying so on failure.

## 4. "Difference from C" is argued; the anti-repeat list is not.

**What is missing.** §11 contrasts C thoroughly and §7/§9 nod once at round-1 `foldout-atlas`.
The anti-repeat list in brief v3 also names round 2 "hand-drawn triage chart" and "editorial
ledger of facts", and round 1 "field atlas map" — the three nearest neighbours to a paper
page of hand-drawn, numbered, annotated plates. None is named, and §8 never explains how the
same `hand-drawn-diagrams` look is kept from re-treading the triage chart.

**Why it matters.** The brief forbids repeating the list and allows borrowing a mechanism but
"never the look". The closest neighbour is the one that decides whether this reads as new —
and it is the one the document does not mention.

**Concrete fix.** Add the missing paragraph: for each near neighbour, name the borrowed
mechanism and the deliberate difference — borrow numbered leader-line annotation; the plate
is a drawing of *one service's own object*, not a chart of a process, not a ledger of facts,
not a map of places; no cross-service routing; the drawing never replaces the state word.

## 5. Warmth and the OpenDesign system are applied as surface, not structure.

**What is wrong.** (a) Every warm moment in §6 is motion — the pencil drawing the connector
line, ≤300ms, removed under `prefers-reduced-motion` — or a one-off doodle; no micro-copy is
actually pinned. Under reduced motion, which the design honours, the warmth disappears, and
animation-discipline warns explicitly against motion as the only signal. (b) Atelier Zero is
reduced to palette, radius and Roman numerals: the system's *mandatory* top metadata strip is
neither used nor replaced, and its most on-thesis print devices — plate numbers, corner
brackets, monospace annotations, "Filed under" — are dropped even though the concept is
literally numbered plates; the Roman numerals are used as static nav decoration rather than
the system's sequential chapter walk. (c) The contrast work in §8 is half-done. White-on-coral
≈3.0:1 is correctly refused (that part is right), but "coral is non-text only" collides with
§8's coral Roman numerals and with any coral state mark or focus ring: coral `#ed6f5c`
measures 2.4:1 on Paper `#efe7d2` and 2.6:1 on Bone `#f7f1de`, under the 3:1 the same sentence
claims; the Roman numerals' colour is never pinned.

**Why it matters.** The brief wants warmth "laced in … the structure stays calm", not
motion-bound, and asks each concept to let its assigned skill and design system *shape* the
concept. Token-borrowing is not that, and an unresolved contrast claim fails the
accessibility-baseline rule §8 says it applied.

**Concrete fix.** Give warmth static homes that survive reduced motion: drawn plate states
that persist (a latched door, a settled sparrow), a plate number per service, and 3–4 exact
kind copy strings pinned in DESIGN.md; keep motion as confirmation only. Adopt the metadata
strip or explicitly reject it in one sentence — it is the natural carrier for the required
status line plus the MOCKUP label at 1440, and it can collapse at 390 so it costs no width —
and put "Plate Nº"/annotation devices on the plates. Pin Roman numerals and any meaningful
mark to Ink-mute (6.1:1 on Paper) or Ink, keep coral decorative only, and write the measured
ratios into §8.
