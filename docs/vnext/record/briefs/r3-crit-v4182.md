## DESIGN.md for v4182

# v4182 — Evening rounds (MOCKUP)

Status: MOCKUP design. Synthetic fixture data only (`docs/vnext/fixture/fixture.json`); nothing here is a real reading and nothing is approved.

## 1. Metaphor in one line, and the main gesture.
Worlds is a house you check on at the end of the day, and each service is a lamp the house keeps lit: **Evening rounds**. The main gesture is **walk the rounds**: read one calm sentence about the house, pick a lamp, and see its owner's app docked beside the facts it shares. A small lamp-keeper character (inline SVG) sits in the shell and stands for the whole world's state.

## 2. Integration model: how a service plugs in, where it lives in the shell, how its facts flow.
Start with `media-shelf` (Media Shelf, `healthy`) and `front-door` (Front Door, `needs_attention`, "Needs a human"). Each is a labelled lamp in the Home register, with its owner named on it (Media, Home). `#add-service` starts Connect for `notes-sync` (Notes Sync, `unavailable`) as five real `<button>` steps: **Discover** (Notes Sync, owned by Memory) → **Credential reference** (a reference name only; a pasted secret is refused with "Worlds keeps a reference, never the secret"; untouched / dirty / submitted-pending states, validated on blur) → **Safe test** (running, then a failure that is honest: reference valid, provider answered "provider timed out"; input kept, "Check provider now" retry, and Publish stays allowed because the local facts are real) → **Preview and choose** (`sync.provider-reachable` `unavailable`, `sync.local-pending` 3 notes `live`, `sync.last-synced` `stale`, one checkbox each, plain text on what will be shared) → **Publish**, where the lamp is lit in the register with the chosen fact ids. Mock only; no provider call.

Home, the service surface and the Agent-facts view all read one in-memory store keyed by fact id. A mock clock starts at the fixture's `generatedAt` and ticks every 4 seconds; ages are recomputed from `checkedAt` ("checked 24s ago" becomes "28s ago"). About every 25 seconds the feed moves `media-shelf` to `stale` (labelled "simulated"), and the owner-app handoff "Check now" settles it. `#simulate-change` applies `lock-back-door` from `crossViewExample`: `frontdoor.back-door` becomes `locked`, Front Door becomes `healthy`, `attentionCount` goes 1 → 0, and Home, the Front Door surface and the Agent-facts row change in the same update, with the lamp-keeper relaxing. Worlds locks nothing; the copy says the owning app reported it. `#disconnect` is visible on the first screen for the selected service (Front Door by default), opens a confirm naming the service with a "Disconnect" button, then states what is gone ("Front Door's lamp, its facts and its app frame are removed; Media Shelf and your shell are untouched").

## 3. Navigation between Worlds and a service (phone and desktop).
The persistent destinations are Home, Connect, Memory and Settings. A service is opened from its lamp; Worlds keeps its chrome (title, MOCKUP label, `#live-toggle`, the rounds sentence) at every depth. Back always returns to the invoking lamp with focus restored. Details in §12.

## 4. The embedded-app frame (sandbox, fallback, what the shell keeps around it).
The owner's app is drawn as a framed, inset screen with a title bar naming the owner and a permanent fallback link: Media Shelf "Open Media app" (`app://media`), Front Door "Open Home app" (`app://home`), Notes Sync "Open Memory app" (`app://memory`). Fixture hrefs only; the brief's `https://example.invalid/...` form may be used where a web fallback is shown. Intended boundary: the frame receives no Worlds credential. Whether a browser enforces that, the sandbox flags and app messaging are UNKNOWN. If the frame will not load, say so in words, keep the fact card and the owner link, and offer a retry. The shell, the fact card, Back and the live state keep working around the frame.

## 5. The agent/human shared-facts model (source, freshness, who sees, who acts).
The fact card beside the frame and the Agent-facts view share records with Home. Each row has source, observed time and freshness word, **Agent can see**, **Human can see**, **Who can act**. `frontdoor.back-door`: agent `read`, human `act`, reason "Locking the door changes the external system and is the person's decision." `frontdoor.guest-code-used`: agent `none` as a named, focusable, disabled control with its exact fixture reason; human `read`. `frontdoor.unlocked-for`: both `read`. Governed actions hand off (approvals to Project Home, others to the owning app); the mock never decides. Notes Sync keeps provider failure and local data separate: provider `unavailable`, pending `live`, last sync `stale`.

## 6. States: empty/first run (zero services, zero providers), quiet, needs-you, stale, unknown, disconnected.
- **First run (zero services, zero providers):** an unlit shelf, the lamp-keeper holding a spare bulb, "Nothing is plugged in yet. Connect your first service when you're ready," `#add-service`, and no invented readings.
- **Quiet:** Media Shelf, `healthy`, steady lamp. **Needs-you:** Front Door, exact state label and `attentionReason` text.
- **Stale:** shade tilted plus the word "Stale" and the age; **Unknown:** outlined dark lamp, the word "Unknown", its fixture reason, never blank. **Unavailable:** lamp with a "cord out" mark and the word.
- **Error:** safe-test failure and frame-load failure each say what happened, why, and what to do, and keep input. Retries back off (2s, 4s, 8s); after three, a copyable error id.
- **Disconnected:** says plainly what is gone; remaining lamps stay usable. Loading uses a still skeleton with a 15s "taking longer" note. Long names and 200-character summaries wrap without breaking layout. Status is always a word plus a shape, never colour alone.

## 7. Borrowed from Homarr / Dashy / HA cards / MCP ext-apps, and what is deliberately different.
Borrow Homarr's one integration feeding UI and assistant, Dashy's open-in-place, HA cards' literal state words, MCP ext-apps' contained UI under host permissions. Different: no widget grid to rearrange, no topology or routes between services, and nothing the shell can decide; the frame is always secondary to the owner label and the facts.

## 8. Your OpenDesign starting point (skill + design system) and how you used it; which guide rules you applied.
Skill `after-hours-editorial-template` is a dark, cinematic video template, so only its mechanics are used: staged reveal of a headline, then supporting line, then detail (on entering a lamp); a short "chapter wipe" between Home and a service, both cut to a plain fade under reduced motion; and its after-hours baseline as an automatic evening theme (`prefers-color-scheme: dark`), not the default. Skipped: grain, vignette, cursor light, magenta accent, 3-second pages, video framing. Its advice to avoid `localStorage` and `confirm` is followed: confirmation is an in-page dialog. Design system `ds-lovable`: cream `#f7f4ed` page, charcoal `#1c1c1c` with opacity-derived greys, `#5f5f5d` secondary text, `#eceae4` passive and `rgba(28,28,28,0.4)` interactive borders, 12px cards, 6px buttons, pills only for toggles, weights 400 and 600 only, 8px spacing, borders instead of shadows, the dark inset-shadow primary button. Fonts are system stacks (Camera Plain is not available and no external fonts are allowed); only the one hero sentence takes `ui-serif` as a nod to the editorial skill (flagged deviation). Lovable has no saturated accent, so state is carried by words and lamp shape and there is no colour accent. Its ring-blue focus at 50% is too faint on cream; use a 2px `#1c1c1c` outline plus its soft shadow, checked at 3:1. Dark theme values are derived from the same tokens, contrast measured on implemented pairs. Guide rules: decision coverage before code, state-coverage's five states plus form states, anti-ai-slop (no emoji icons, no gradient hero, no invented metrics, no lorem), animation-discipline (state-change motion only, under 300ms, off under reduced motion), accessibility baseline (44px targets, keyboard, live regions created before use).

## 9. Signature element, hero thesis, reference lock.
Signature: the lamp-keeper and the lamp row. One SVG character (a small lamp with a face-less, shade-for-a-head silhouette) and one lamp per service, whose shape changes with state and which glows gently on a change ("connected" is a lamp being switched on, a 400ms warm-up that becomes a still glow under reduced motion). Hero thesis: "Every light in the house, and who looks after it." Reference lock: Apple Home's accessory-to-home status summary and Linear's calm, keyboard-first shell (mechanics only; no palettes copied).

## 10. Risks and UNKNOWNs.
Embed support and isolation, Connect API and protocol, disconnect history, and whether simulated stale events misread as real. Whimsy could crowd a calm app, so there is one character and one lamp set, and no confetti. Serif hero versus Lovable's humanist sans is a deliberate deviation. Contrast must be measured on built pairs in both themes. Fixture data is synthetic.

## 11. How it differs from C, and what it keeps
Keeps from C: the persistent Home / Connect / Memory / Settings shell, owner-labelled places in a fixed reading order, the owning app docked beside a shared fact card, the exact fixture facts, permission rows and handoff wording, honest separated states, and the Connect lifecycle for `notes-sync`. Differs: C's bays are a static, flat register with no motion; this concept adds a character and a lamp for each service, a live clock that visibly ages and stales facts, and a connected moment. C's physical metaphor (a cabinet bezel) is replaced by a domestic one (lamps and an evening check), a warm cream and evening theme replaces its white/orange skeuomorphic system, and a Home sentence ("one thing needs you") leads in place of a bare register. Connect is five steps rather than six (credential and test are kept, preview and choose merged). C's rule that nothing animates is relaxed to calm, state-driven motion that reduces to still states.

## 12. Desktop and mobile layouts
**Desktop (1440px; tablet around 768px is a bonus).** Three columns. A left rail holds the lamp-keeper, Home / Connect / Memory / Settings, and the MOCKUP label. The centre column holds the rounds sentence and the lamp register (all services in fixed order, each with state word, age and owner). The right column holds the selected service: header with `#disconnect` and the owner link, the framed app, and the docked fact card. The top strip holds `#simulate-change` and `#live-toggle` (ON by default, "Live, checking every 4s" / "Paused"). On first screen the selected Front Door shows with `#disconnect` visible. At about 768px the rail shrinks to icon plus label and the detail column moves below the register.

**Mobile (390px, designed separately).** One column, no sideways scroll, 44px targets. A bottom tab bar holds Home, Connect, Memory, Settings. Reading order on first screen: MOCKUP label and `#live-toggle` header, the rounds sentence, a compact selected-service card (Front Door: state word, reason, `#disconnect`, "Open Home app"), then the mock controls with `#simulate-change`, then the remaining lamps. Opening a lamp raises a full-screen sheet with Back; its order is state and facts first, "what you can do" next, and the framed app last, collapsed to a single "Show the app here" button above its fallback link, because facts matter more than a small embedded screen. `#add-service` is a button in the Home header and at the end of the lamp list; Connect steps are a vertical stack with one step visible, a sticky Back/Next row above the tab bar.

Checker ids: `#add-service`, `#disconnect` (visible on first screen, confirm button "Disconnect"), `#simulate-change`, `#live-toggle`, and a visible MOCKUP label.
