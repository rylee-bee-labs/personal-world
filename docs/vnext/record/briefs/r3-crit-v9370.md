## DESIGN.md for v9370

# v9370 — The service workbench

## 1. Metaphor in one line, and the main gesture.
Worlds is a welcoming workbench with clearly named service stations; choose a station to see its own app alongside the facts Worlds shares. The main gesture is **select a service, then move between its app and the shared facts without losing your place**. Reading this as a daily personal application for one person, with calm structure, small crafted warmth, and a restrained workshop language. OpenDesign dials: variance 5, motion 2, density 4.

## 2. Integration model: how a service plugs in, where it lives in the shell, how its facts flow.
Start with exactly `media-shelf` (Media Shelf, `healthy`) and `front-door` (Front Door, `needs_attention`); select Front Door by default so its state and `#disconnect` are visible immediately. `#add-service` starts Notes Sync (`notes-sync`, `unavailable`) through Discover → Credential reference → Safe test → Preview → Choose what to publish → Publish. Accept a reference, never a secret; test failure is explicit and offers retry or back, while preserving the fixture’s local notes. Preview `sync.provider-reachable` (`unavailable`), `sync.local-pending` (3 notes, `live`), and `sync.last-synced` (`stale`). Facts flow by ID from one in-memory store to Home, the service station and Agent facts. `#simulate-change` applies the fixture’s `lock-back-door` example: `frontdoor.back-door` becomes `locked`, Front Door becomes `healthy`, attention count becomes 0, and all three views update together. This is a mock handoff/review only; Worlds does not operate the lock. Disconnect confirms with a button labelled “Disconnect”, removes the selected station and embedded app, and leaves the shell and other stations available.

## 3. Navigation between Worlds and a service (phone and desktop).
Persistent primary destinations are Home, Connect, Memory and Settings; service stations are reached from Home and Connect. On desktop, a narrow left navigation rail stays put while Home’s service register and selected service workspace fill the main area. Service actions belong to the owning app; approvals hand off to Project Home. On mobile, a fixed bottom bar holds the four Worlds destinations; selecting a station opens its own full-screen detail with a clear Back control and a reading order of title/state → owner app → facts → actions. Preserve semantic source order, 44px targets, visible keyboard focus and safe-area padding. Settings includes live-feed preference and motion comfort.

## 4. The embedded-app frame (sandbox, fallback, what the shell keeps around it).
Show the owner’s app in a bounded, clearly labelled frame beside or above the Worlds-owned fact surface. Keep Worlds navigation, service name/state, and the owner fallback visible around it: “Open Media app” (`app://media`), “Open Home app” (`app://home`), or “Open Memory app” (`app://memory`). If an embedded app cannot load, state that plainly and retain facts plus fallback. No credential value enters the frame. Exact embed protocol and browser-enforced isolation remain UNKNOWN; no mock action claims a real external change.

## 5. The agent/human shared-facts model (source, freshness, who sees, who acts).
Every fact row shows its literal value or explicit `unknown`/`unavailable`, source, checked/freshness wording, and separate “Agent can see”, “Human can see”, and “Who can act” fields. `frontdoor.back-door` is agent-read/human-act; show its fixture reason and route action to Project Home. `frontdoor.guest-code-used` shows human-read and a named disabled agent access control with its exact fixture reason, never exposing `visitor-3` to the agent. Other fixture permissions remain independent. Agent facts is a real view of these same records, not a second store. The live feed starts on; `#live-toggle` visibly pauses/resumes age updates, and a modest tick may make a service stale. State words accompany every visual cue; reduced motion removes transitions, not data updates.

## 6. States: empty/first run (zero services, zero providers), quiet, needs-you, stale, unknown, disconnected.
First run shows no invented readings, a local Worlds/Memory baseline, and a clear Connect invitation; zero providers means zero provider facts. Quiet uses Media Shelf’s fixture summary. Needs-you uses Front Door’s exact summary and attention reason. Notes Sync test failure keeps “local notes still work” distinct from provider unavailable. Stale, unknown, unavailable and not configured are explicit words with fixture explanations; blanks never imply health. Disconnect names what left and says its app is no longer available here; history retention is UNKNOWN. A small inline SVG workbench sprout marks a successful connection, with kind copy (“All set; Notes Sync has a place here.”), never status by illustration alone. MOCKUP is always visible. Default state is low-stimulation; no shimmer or looping motion.

## 7. Borrowed from Homarr / Dashy / HA cards / MCP ext-apps, and what is deliberately different.
Borrow Homarr’s one integration feeding multiple surfaces, Dashy’s in-place app opening, Home Assistant cards’ readable state, and MCP ext-apps’ contained app boundary. Keep service ownership explicit and facts shared across human and agent views. The workbench uses a linear register and one selected station, not a dashboard widget wall, a map, or a cabinet of repeated bays; it makes the small act of pairing an owner app with its shared facts the organizing gesture.

## 8. Your OpenDesign starting point (skill + design system) and how you used it; which guide rules you applied.
Starting point: `skill-taste-skill.md` + `ds-skeumorphism.md`. The skill says its marketing-page patterns do not govern dense product UI or multi-step forms, so borrow its anti-default and craft discipline only; this brief and Worlds contracts govern application structure. Use skeuomorphism’s playful physical cue as one flat, labeled work surface and a tiny inline SVG sprout, not textured panels or pressable fake hardware. Keep its spacing/type scale and `#111827` text; treat `#FA3C00` as a restrained accent only after contrast review, never as the sole state signal. Avoid large pure-white surfaces per Accessibility contract; use a soft, low-glare neutral field. Apply one consistent shape system, concise fixture copy, visible focus, static loading, named states, 44px targets, keyboard access, 390px reflow and reduced-motion behavior. No external fonts/images, invented values, gradients, emoji, or decorative motion.

## 9. Signature element, hero thesis, reference lock.
Signature: the paired “owner app / shared facts” work surface, joined by a narrow labeled seam that makes ownership and shared truth legible at a glance. Hero thesis: “Every service keeps its own hands; Worlds keeps the shared picture.” Reference lock: Homarr 2.0’s integration reused across surfaces, and Apple Home’s accessory-to-home state handoff; borrow mechanics, not their visual language.

## 10. Risks and UNKNOWNs.
Connect discovery protocol, credential-reference format, actual embed isolation, service messaging, and disconnect history behavior are UNKNOWN. The fixture is synthetic; label the concept MOCKUP and do not imply readings are real. Check text, control, and focus contrast in the eventual implementation. App handoffs and the lock example are demonstrative only.

## 11. How it differs from C, and what it keeps
C’s cabinet bays become a continuous workbench register and an owner/facts workspace, so the signature is the labeled seam joining an app to its shared facts, not a cabinet or inset bezel. It keeps C’s persistent Home / Connect / Memory / Settings shell, explicit service ownership, docked owning app, shared fact card, honest state words, and calm focus on one selected service. Warmth comes from a tiny sprout and humane confirmation copy rather than changing the application’s structure.

## 12. Desktop and mobile layouts
At 1440px, keep a 224px navigation rail, a roughly 300px service register, and a flexible workspace with the embedded owner app beside the shared-facts panel; keep service title, state, and `#disconnect` visible on the first screen. At 390px, use the four-item bottom navigation, a single-column Home register, then a dedicated service detail with Back; place the owner app first and facts immediately after, with actions including `#disconnect` visible without opening a secondary panel. `#add-service` is a prominent Connect action, while `#simulate-change` and `#live-toggle` remain visible in the current service/facts view. No sideways scrolling; long values wrap, controls stay at least 44px, and the facts panel follows the app in reading order. At both widths, show `#add-service`, `#disconnect`, `#simulate-change`, `#live-toggle`, and the MOCKUP label in their relevant first-screen context; on Home, `#disconnect` targets the initially selected Front Door service.
