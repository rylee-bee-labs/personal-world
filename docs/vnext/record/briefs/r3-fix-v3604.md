## Your DESIGN.md

# v3604 — The service shelf

## 1. Metaphor in one line, and the main gesture.
A calm service shelf gives each independent app a labeled place, while a shared “what Worlds knows” slip follows the selected service. The main gesture is **choose a service, then compare its own screen with the facts Worlds shares**. A small hand-drawn paper tab marked “from this app” adds warmth without turning the interface into a storybook.

## 2. Integration model: how a service plugs in, where it lives in the shell, how its facts flow.
Start with `media-shelf` (Media Shelf, `healthy`) and `front-door` (Front Door, `needs_attention`); their owner links and fixture facts remain literal. `#add-service` opens a six-step Connect flow for `notes-sync` (Notes Sync, `unavailable`): discover; enter a credential reference (never a secret); safe test; preview `sync.provider-reachable`, `sync.local-pending` and `sync.last-synced`; choose facts to publish; publish. Each service owns its data and actions. Worlds composes selected facts into Home and Agent facts from one in-memory fixture store. `#simulate-change` applies the fixture’s `lock-back-door` example to `frontdoor.back-door`, updating Home, the Front Door surface and Agent facts together; it is a mock handoff/review, never a real lock action. `#disconnect` confirms the service by name and removes its shelf entry while other Worlds destinations remain usable.

## 3. Navigation between Worlds and a service (phone and desktop).
Desktop keeps Home, Connect, Memory and Settings in a persistent left rail. A compact service index sits beside the selected service’s framed app, with the Worlds-owned fact slip adjacent and visibly separate. On mobile, a persistent bottom bar carries those four destinations; the service index becomes a list screen and a selected service gets a detail screen with an explicit Back button. The reading order remains navigation, main content, then complementary facts. No critical action moves into hover or overflow.

## 4. The embedded-app frame (sandbox, fallback, what the shell keeps around it).
The owner app sits in a distinct, restrained frame labeled with service name, state and “owned by” app. The host navigation, current service state and shared fact slip stay outside the frame. Show fixture fallback links (`Open Media app`, `Open Home app`, `Open Memory app`) at the frame edge, including when the embed is unavailable. A missing or failed frame leaves its explanation and owner link in place. The mock frame receives no Worlds credential; browser isolation, sandbox flags and app messaging remain UNKNOWN. Decisions hand off to Project Home for governed Home operations, and to the owner app for other service actions.

## 5. The agent/human shared-facts model (source, freshness, who sees, who acts).
Home, the service detail and Agent facts read the same fact IDs. Every fact row states its source, checked/observed time, freshness in words, who can see it, and who can act. Preserve the fixture distinction on `frontdoor.back-door`: Agent can read, Human can act, because locking changes the external system. Keep `frontdoor.guest-code-used` human-readable; show Agent “none” and its fixture reason without exposing `visitor-3`. On the live Front Door screen, `#disconnect` is visible on the first screen. A gentle live tick updates “checked … ago” everywhere, may mark one service stale, and settles optimistic action feedback visibly. `#live-toggle` is on by default and pauses/resumes that feed; data continues under reduced motion, while decorative motion stops.

## 6. States: empty/first run (zero services, zero providers), quiet, needs-you, stale, unknown, disconnected.
Zero services offers a small drawn empty shelf, says no services are connected, and points to Connect; zero providers means no readings are invented. Quiet uses Media Shelf’s exact `healthy` state. Needs-you preserves Front Door’s `needs_attention` label and reason. Stale names what is old and when it was checked; unknown says no current observation, as for Garden Camera. Unavailable remains distinct from stale: Notes Sync’s provider can be unavailable while three local notes remain live. Disconnected explains which service left. Connect also has loading, validation, failed safe-test, empty preview and long-copy states, each with cause and recovery; no spinner without a timeout. Status always has words as well as restrained semantic color.

## 7. Borrowed from Homarr / Dashy / HA cards / MCP ext-apps, and what is deliberately different.
Borrow Homarr’s one integration reused across surfaces, Dashy’s in-place app access, Home Assistant cards’ shared state, and MCP ext-apps’ contained UI boundary. The shelf makes source and ownership explicit at the moment facts are compared; it does not turn services into a map, cable patchbay or floating window collection. Worlds frames and composes; the service remains its owner.

## 8. Your OpenDesign starting point (skill + design system) and how you used it; which guide rules you applied.
Starting point: `skill-minimalist-skill.md` and `ds-storytelling.md`. Use the minimalist skill’s flat editorial hierarchy and restrained warm monochrome as structure; use Storytelling’s playful voice and one hand-drawn paper tab as the small narrative accent. Keep its spacing scale (4/8/12/16/24/32), text token `#111827`, and use the named palette tokens only where they remain readable; reject its Inter/large-white-surface defaults where they conflict with the minimalist rulebook and low-glare contract. Apply anti-slop rules (specific fixture copy, no gradients, emoji or invented metrics), color/typography contrast and hierarchy, state coverage, labeled native controls, and Laws of UX grouping/proximity. Use the craft rulebooks’ 44px targets, visible focus, semantic order, static default feedback, and reduced-motion behavior. Any final color pair must meet WCAG AA before implementation.

## 9. Signature element, hero thesis, reference lock.
Signature: the “from this app” paper tab visually bridges a framed owner app to its shared fact slip, and repeats on Home and Agent facts. Hero thesis: **“Every service keeps its own screen; every shared fact keeps its source.”** Reference lock: Homarr 2.0’s reusable integration mechanic and Apple Home’s accessory-to-home state relationship; borrow the relationship, not their styling.

## 10. Risks and UNKNOWNs.
Real Connect protocol, provider sandbox enforcement, app messaging, disconnect history retention and exact live freshness policy are UNKNOWN. The fixture is synthetic; label the prototype MOCKUP and never imply a real reading. The paper illustration must not carry status meaning. Verify text/component contrast, 390px reflow without horizontal scroll, 44px targets, keyboard/focus behavior and reduced-motion treatment in the built artifact.

## 11. How it differs from C, and what it keeps
C’s fixed cabinet bays and docked app-plus-fact pairing are the starting point. v3604 shifts the visual thesis from a cabinet of places to a **service shelf with a source tab**: the small “from this app” marker is the single signature, and the shelf/list is intentionally light rather than a physical cabinet. It keeps the real Worlds shell, clear service ownership, honest state labels, owner fallback, shared facts and an embedded app framed by persistent Worlds navigation. It does not reuse C’s inset-bezel cabinet treatment.

## 12. Desktop and mobile layouts
At 1440px, use a persistent left navigation rail, a narrow service index, and a generous selected-service region split between the owner frame and adjacent fact slip; keep the first-screen service state and `#disconnect` visible without opening another surface. Home and Agent facts reuse the same fact-row grammar. At 390px, use the bottom navigation, then a single-column service index/detail flow; the detail places service name/state, `#disconnect`, owner frame and facts in that order, with Connect and `#simulate-change` reachable as labeled full-width actions. Keep `#live-toggle` visible near freshness. The Connect steps become one-column screens with explicit Back/Continue buttons. All targets are at least 44px, safe areas are respected, and content reflows at 200% zoom with no sideways page scroll.

# v3604 critique — round 3

Critique of `docs/vnext/round3/v3604/DESIGN.md` against `concept-brief-v3.md` (which carries all of `concept-brief-v2.md` forward), the decisions in force, and `fixture/fixture.json`. Five gaps, most important first.

---

## 1. The difference from C is a rename, not a design change

**What is wrong.** Brief v3 §"The direction" is explicit: "**Use C as the starting direction, do not clone it.**" Every concept's DESIGN.md "must say in one paragraph how it differs from C, and what it keeps" — and v3604 §11 does say it. But what it *keeps* is the entire layout, and what it *changes* is a noun and a garnish.

Compare the two directly:

| | C (`direction-C-DESIGN.md`) | v3604 |
|---|---|---|
| Desktop frame | persistent rail Home/Connect/Memory/Settings; register beside selected bay; app frame docked next to the shared fact card | §3 persistent left rail, same four destinations; service index beside the selected service; owner frame beside the fact slip |
| 390px | register becomes a one-column list; selecting a bay replaces it with a one-column detail; persistent destinations + explicit Back | §3 / §12 the index becomes a list screen; a selected service gets a detail screen with an explicit Back button |
| Signature | inset screen + fact card docked together, owner/fallback permanently visible | §9 the "from this app" tab bridging frame to fact slip |

The dock, the register→detail substitution and the pairing — the three moves C says it "earns its metaphor" with (`direction-C-DESIGN.md` §7) — are all reproduced. "Shelf" for "cabinet" is a synonym in a shelf-adjacent product; §11's own admission that it "does not reuse C's inset-bezel cabinet treatment" is a surface-level distinction, and the empty-state "small drawn empty shelf" (§6) walks it back toward C's "vacant bay" anyway.

**Why it matters.** The round exists to produce directions that can be compared. A concept that is C with a lighter bezel is not a direction; it makes the comparison meaningless and wastes a build slot. It also weakens the signature on its own terms: §10 concedes "the paper illustration must not carry status meaning", so the one new element is explicitly decorative, while the design-quality guide's own test is "the hero is a thesis."

**Fix.** Change the *information architecture*, not the vocabulary, and say which of C's structural moves you break. The strongest available break is C's §2/§9 pinned invariant — "fixed reading order", "a fixed owner register, not a map". Make the shelf **ordered by need, not by name**: the index sorts needs-you first, then least-recently-checked, and **reorders visibly when the live tick moves a service to stale**, with the reordering itself animated calmly and made inert under reduced motion (the order still changes, announced in words: "Front Door moved to the top — checked 45s ago"). Then state in §11, in the required paragraph, that this live need-ordering is the deliberate break from C's fixed register and why it earns the shelf metaphor. Keep the source tab, but make it do work: it is the only control that opens the fact slip, so provenance becomes a step rather than a caption.

## 2. `#disconnect` is not on the first screen at 390px

**What is wrong.** Brief v3's checker contract: "`#disconnect` is **visible on the first screen** for the currently selected service (not hidden until something is opened), and asks for confirmation with a button labelled 'Disconnect'." v3604 §3 says that on mobile "the service index becomes a list screen and a selected service gets a detail screen". §12 repeats it. So the first screen at 390px is the **index**, where no service is selected and there is nothing to disconnect. §5 then asserts the opposite — "On the live Front Door screen, `#disconnect` is visible on the first screen" — and the document never reconciles §3/§12 with §5, leaving the build to pick.

**Why it matters.** This is the one requirement in the whole brief with an automated check, and it will fail at 390px as written. Beyond the checker, it is the thing that makes the shell feel like a real phone app: you can leave a service without hunting for it, and §6's "disconnected explains which service left" has a natural trigger. It also has an accessibility edge — on a 390 screen the first screen is what the phone user and any screen-reader user lands on.

**Fix.** Pin the mobile landing route explicitly in §12: **"at 390 the app opens on the Front Door detail screen — it is the only `needs_attention` service — with Back returning to the index"**, and state the `attentionCount: 0` consequence lands on Home at the same moment. Keep the same named confirmation dialog (confirm by service name, button labelled exactly "Disconnect") from both the detail screen and, as a fallback, a per-row control in the index. Say what happens after: from the detail screen the phone returns to the index with the "Front Door left the shelf" notice still in place. While you are in §12, note that §3 pins Back/Continue stepper buttons only for the phone — name the six Connect stepper labels ("Back", "Continue", "Connect Notes Sync") as real `<button>`s at every width, since the checker asks for that.

## 3. Fixture fidelity is asserted, not pinned — the promised states are unreachable and the permission floor is unnamed

**What is wrong.** Several claims point at data the design never puts on the shelf, and one label is wrong.

- §2 pins the composed set to `media-shelf`, `front-door`, `notes-sync`. But §6 offers the `unknown` state "as for Garden Camera" and says "zero providers means no readings are invented" — **Garden Camera and `local-weather` are never composed**, so `unknown` and the zero-provider/empty state are states the prototype claims in prose and can never render. The only fixture service with zero facts is `local-weather` (`not_configured`, with an `emptyReason` written for exactly this), and the brief's protected invariant is zero-provider usability.
- §6 says "Quiet uses Media Shelf's exact `healthy` state" and "Needs-you preserves Front Door's `needs_attention` label". Those are the raw state tokens. The fixture supplies `stateLabel` values — Media Shelf is **"Healthy"**, Front Door is **"Needs a human"** (with `attentionReason`) — and brief v2 §2 requires "its ids, names and states verbatim". Use the label the fixture gives you, and quote `attentionReason` as the reason line.
- §5 never transcribes the fact rows. `frontdoor.unlocked-for` (47 minutes) is dropped; §4 lists fallback *labels* but not the `app://` hrefs. And the `guest-code-used` rule is left ambiguous: "keep it human-readable … without exposing `visitor-3`" leaves open whether the human column shows `visitor-3` (the fixture gives human `read`). Critically, the fixture's permission rule says `none` "must render as a named, disabled control with its reason, never hidden" — v3604 never says the Agent `none` is a control at all, only that it is shown.

**Why it matters.** This is the honesty floor. A prototype that narrates states it cannot show is the exact failure the fixture's `statesVocabulary.rule` was written to prevent ("A blank value is never a state"), and the brief v2 floors put stale/unknown-in-words and agent/human permissions on the checklist. A disabled-with-reason control is also the pattern that makes the permission model visible rather than decorative.

**Fix.** Add a literal fixture table to §2 or §6 — id, name, `stateLabel`, and every fact id + value + state — and compose four services so the states are reachable on the real first screen: `media-shelf` (quiet), `front-door` (needs-you), `garden-camera` (`unknown`, with `unknownReason` verbatim: "The device stopped answering at the fixture's baseline; no source has answered since."), and `notes-sync` (added through Connect, `unavailable`). Give `local-weather` the zero-provider slot — a shelf entry with zero facts that renders its `emptyReason` and invents no reading — or drop the "zero providers" sentence from §6 entirely rather than leaving it unbacked. For permissions, write the column split in words: human reads `visitor-3` on `frontdoor.guest-code-used`; the Agent cell is a **named, disabled, focusable control labelled "Agent cannot read this" carrying the verbatim `agentReason`** ("Identifying a person is human-only in this fixture; the agent sees that a guest code was used, not who."), never a hidden cell. Add `frontdoor.unlocked-for` (47 minutes, agent `read`, human `read`) as the second row of the fact-grammar example so the four permissions appear at least once each.

## 4. Reactivity has no schedule, no named optimistic action, and no transition spec

**What is wrong.** Brief v3 §4 asks for: a gentle tick that "changes freshness ('checked 2s ago' → '12s ago'), occasionally moves a service to stale, and updates every view that shows it without a reload"; "Actions give instant optimistic feedback and settle visibly"; "Transitions between states are visible (and calm under reduced motion)"; and a visible pause/resume.

v3604 §5 delivers one sentence for all of it: "A gentle live tick updates 'checked … ago' everywhere, may mark one service to stale, and settles optimistic action feedback visibly." There is no interval, no rule for which service goes stale or what it goes stale *from*, and — the fatal part — no recovery. A monotonic decay can only get more stale, so after the first half-minute every string in the product reads "checked 90s ago", the front door chip goes grey and stays there, and the demo dies on its own. "Optimistic feedback" names no action at all: `#simulate-change` is described in §2 as "a mock handoff/review" with no pending phase, and `#disconnect` and Connect-publish — the two actions with an obvious optimistic shape — have none.

**Why it matters.** This is the round's headline addition ("Reactive", and the owner's decision: "they seem to not quite get the fact it needs to be integrated"). Requirements 1-6 are otherwise well covered in this DESIGN.md; reactivity is the part that is currently a claim rather than a specification, and it is the part a reviewer can most easily watch fail live.

**Fix.** Pin a small table in §5 and reference it from §12. Suggested schedule: tick every 3s, recomputing age from `checkedAt` rather than a stored counter; at 45s Front Door's facts flip to `stale` and the service chip reads "checked 45s ago"; at 60s a re-check returns it to `live` with a one-line "re-checked just now" note — so the feed demonstrates a full live → stale → live cycle, which is what proves reactivity rather than decay. Name the optimistic action and its three phases: `#simulate-change` immediately sets `frontdoor.back-door` to a pending "locking…" row on Home, on the Front Door surface **and** in Agent facts in one tick, then settles after ~900ms to `locked` / `healthy` / `attentionCount: 0`. Do the same, shorter, for `#disconnect` ("Disconnecting Front Door…" → entry leaves the index with a calm fade → notice lands) and for Connect publish. Say which transitions are the only animated ones (state chip, row settle, index reorder from §1) and that reduced motion turns them into instant swaps while the **tick keeps running** — data under reduced motion, not the data frozen. Finally, make pause legible: with `#live-toggle` off, every freshness line switches to "feed paused · last checked 45s ago" in words, not just a number that stops counting.

## 5. Warmth is one repeated garnish, and the design-system binding names the wrong system's tokens

**What is wrong.** Brief v3 §2 names four kinds of warmth to lace in: "kind micro-copy, a tiny illustration or character accent drawn in inline SVG, a gentle motion on a state change, **a satisfying 'connected' moment**." v3604 supplies exactly one — the "from this app" paper tab (§1, §9) — plus an unnamed "small drawn empty shelf" (§6). There is no micro-copy rule anywhere in the document, and §2's step 6 is the bare word "publish": the moment the brief most wants is the one place the design says nothing about.

§8's OpenDesign binding is also incoherent on its own terms. The headline claim is "the minimalist skill's flat editorial hierarchy and **restrained warm monochrome**" — that palette is the skill's: canvas `#F7F6F3`/`#FBFBFA`, surface `#FFFFFF`/`#F9F9F8`, structural border `#EAEAEA`, text `#111111`/`#2F3437`, secondary `#787774`, radius 8/12px, `1px solid #EAEAEA` cards. But the only token v3604 actually names is `#111827` — which is **ds-storytelling's** text token, as is the 4/8/12/16/24/32 spacing scale it keeps. So the concept announces a warm-monochrome system and quotes none of its values, then quotes the other system's. Brief v2 is blunt about this lever: "Bind to a design system (lever 1): each concept has an assigned design system; do not invent values outside it."

**Why it matters.** "Whimsy and warmth laced in" is the decision in force, and one garnish repeated on three surfaces is not laced in — it is a theme. Meanwhile the binding is the part of the brief a reviewer checks most mechanically: if every named value comes from the file you did not claim, the binding is decorative too. §10's own "the paper illustration must not carry status meaning" then leaves the signature with nothing to do.

**Fix.** In §1 and §9, specify four warmth moments with placement and exact copy — the Connect success ("Notes Sync is on the shelf. Three local notes are still live; the provider is not answering.", with a small drawn shelf-tab settling into the index at ~200ms), a stale nudge ("Nothing has heard from Garden Camera since 11:02."), a first-run line ("Nothing is connected yet. Worlds will not guess."), and one settle on a state change — and state that all four degrade to a static swap under reduced motion and none of them encode status. In §8, name the tokens you will actually use and say which file each came from: warm canvas `#F7F6F3`, `#FFFFFF` restricted to small frame interiors, `1px solid #EAEAEA`, radius ≤8px, text `#111111`/`#2F3437` with `#787774` secondary — plus the one place you keep a Storytelling token (e.g. `#111827` for the fact-slip label, or the 4/8/12/16/24/32 scale) — and state why the other system's values were rejected. Also keep and apply the skill's bans it does not currently mention: no `rounded-full` containers, no shadows above 0.05 opacity, no emoji (the tabs and labels must be inline SVG or text).
