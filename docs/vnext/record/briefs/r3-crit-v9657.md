## DESIGN.md for v9657

# v9657 — Duty board

MOCKUP. Nothing here is approved and nothing here is a real reading. Single self-contained `index.html`, hand-tuned CSS, vanilla JS, no frameworks, no CDN, no external fonts or images.

## 1. Metaphor in one line, and the main gesture.

Worlds is the duty board by the front door: every service you have connected has one shift card on it, and the main gesture is **read the board, then pick a card up**. Reading never requires a decision and never makes one. The board is a real daily application, not a diagram of services.

## 2. Integration model: how a service plugs in, where it lives in the shell, how its facts flow.

First render holds exactly `media-shelf` (Media Shelf, `healthy`) and `front-door` (Front Door, `needs_attention`), in that fixture reading order. Both own their own data and their own actions; Worlds holds one in-memory store keyed by fact ID (`media.items`, `frontdoor.back-door`, …) and Home, the card, the service screen and Memory all read from it — never from a copy.

`#add-service` (a primary button in the Home header and the only action in Connect's empty state) runs the six-step lifecycle as real `<button>`s with labels: **Discover → Credential reference → Safe test → Preview → Choose → Publish**. Discover lists the remaining fixture services with their verbatim state and owner; `notes-sync` (Notes Sync, `unavailable`, owner Memory) is the designed path and the others render disabled with the reason "This prototype demonstrates the lifecycle with Notes Sync" rather than half-built branches. The credential step accepts a reference only and rejects a secret value with the line "Worlds stores a credential reference, never the secret", validating on blur with untouched / dirty-valid / submitted-pending states and preserving what you typed on failure. Preview lists `sync.provider-reachable` (`unavailable`), `sync.local-pending` (3 notes, `live`) and `sync.last-synced` (`stale`); the safe test fails with the fixture's `provider timed out` plus a real Retry with 2 s / 4 s backoff and "Last attempted: Xs ago". Publish writes only the ticked fact IDs, presses the new card's ON DUTY stamp once, and says plainly that Notes Sync is on the board and is not answering. `local-weather` (Local Weather, `not_configured`, zero facts) appears in Discover as the zero-provider case with its `emptyReason` verbatim and no invented value.

## 3. Navigation between Worlds and a service (phone and desktop).

Persistent shell destinations **Home · Connect · Memory · Settings** in both layouts, matching the product's own architecture. Desktop: a left nav rail, the board in the main column, a right column holding the live summary and feed controls. Home keeps a selection — `front-door` at first paint, because it is the one that needs a human — and `#disconnect` sits in the Home header beside the selected service's name, **visible on the first screen without opening anything**, opening an `alertdialog` that names Front Door and offers "Disconnect" and "Keep it". Each card also carries its own Disconnect in its detail screen. Opening a card is a route, not a mode: Memory, Settings and the board stay reachable, focus moves to the card heading and returns to the invoking card.

## 4. The embedded-app frame (sandbox, fallback, what the shell keeps around it).

The service screen shows the owning app inside a bounded frame with a permanent lip naming the owner and the fixture's fallback link verbatim — Media Shelf "Open Media app" (`app://media`), Front Door "Open Home app" (`app://home`), Notes Sync "Open Memory app" (`app://memory`). The frame receives no Worlds credential and stays visually subordinate to the card; the shell's nav, back, stamp and Disconnect keep working around it. If embedding fails, the shell and facts stay, the failure is stated in words, and the owner link is offered. Approvals hand off to Project Home, everything else to the owning app; nothing is decided in Worlds. Sandbox flags, browser-enforced isolation and app messaging are UNKNOWN.

## 5. The agent/human shared-facts model (source, freshness, who sees, who acts).

Every fact row carries five separable things: **source** (local / external / none), **checked** (recomputed age from `checkedAt`, never the fixture's `ageSeconds`), the state word, **who can see** (agent / human), and **who can act**. `frontdoor.back-door` is agent `read`, human `act`, with the fixture reason "Locking the door changes the external system and is the person's decision"; the governed path is a mock `lock-back-door` that hands off to Project Home and records mock review only. `frontdoor.guest-code-used` is agent `none` and renders as a named, focusable, disabled control with the fixture reason verbatim: "Identifying a person is human-only in this fixture; the agent sees that a guest code was used, not who." Hidden is never the answer to "not allowed". Memory is the full table of the same records; the shared card, Home and the service screen are the same records rendered smaller.

## 6. States: empty/first run, quiet, needs-you, stale, unknown, disconnected.

**First run / zero services:** a vacant board, "No services yet. Worlds has nothing to say until you give it something to watch", and `#add-service` as the primary CTA — reachable from Settings → "Show the first-run board", so the state exists in the artifact, not in a claim. **Zero providers:** Local Weather contributes no facts and says why. **Quiet:** Media Shelf's `healthy`; the live summary reads "Nothing is wrong. That is the whole report." **Needs-you:** Front Door's `needs_attention` plus its `attentionReason`. **Stale:** the last check did not come back — the last known reading is shown with its original `checkedAt`, never refreshed silently. **Unknown:** Garden Camera's `unknown` and its `unknownReason`; a blank is never a state. **Unavailable:** Notes Sync stays `unavailable` while `sync.local-pending` stays `live` — provider failure is never collapsed into local-data failure. **Disconnected:** the card leaves the board, a plain line says which service is gone, and the remaining cards plus every shell destination keep working. Every surface also carries loading (skeleton plus a 15 s "taking longer than expected"), error-with-recovery, and a long-copy edge case.

## 7. Borrowed from Homarr / Dashy / HA cards / MCP ext-apps, and what is deliberately different.

Borrowed: Homarr's one integration feeding several surfaces, Dashy's per-item checked-at indicator, HA cards' literal state words and named disabled controls, Appsmith's bindings-as-a-reaction-graph, MCP ext-apps' host-mediated permission check and contained UI. Deliberately different: the board is a fixed reading order with no map, no route tracing, no patchbay, no unfolding place and no topology; the signature is a physical check-in, not a window floating over anything; the app frame is subordinate chrome, not the hero; and the agent's view is a table of the same records the person reads, never a separate "for the machine" surface.

## 8. OpenDesign starting point (skill + design system) and the guide rules applied.

Starting point: `skill-swiss-creative-mode-template` + `ds-miro`. The skill is a presentation-deck skill, so its look is refused and its mechanics are taken: the **hotspot toggle** becomes Settings → "Explain this board", which reveals inline annotations for provenance and permissions on demand (complexity hidden by default, available to anyone); its **palette cycle** is deliberately refused, because cycling colour would destroy honest state; its self-contained single-file discipline and checklist validation are kept. `ds-miro` supplies the tokens: canvas `#ffffff`, text `#1c1c1e`, secondary `#555a6a`, border `#c7cad5` with the `rgb(224,226,232) 0 0 0 1px` ring shadow, one accent `#5b76fe` for the primary action and the focus ring only (two visible uses per screen), one semantic `#00b473`, the radius ladder 8 / 12 / 24 and a 4-step spacing scale. Pastels are the stamp inks only — coral `#ffc6c6`, teal `#c3faf5`, rose `#ffd8f4` — and never more than two pastel carriers per card, one of which is always the word. Applied: anti-ai-slop (no indigo, no two-stop hero gradient, no emoji icons, no invented metrics, no filler copy, no coloured left-border cards), color (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text, components and focus), typography (seven sizes, three weights 400 / 510 / 600, `0.06em`+ tracking on caps, `-0.02em` on display, `65ch` measure, declared fallback chains, one system family used at several weights because the stack rule forbids webfonts), state-coverage (all five states per surface, validate on blur, retry backoff, `aria-live` regions present before injection), accessibility-baseline (native controls, landmarks, visible `:focus-visible` at a 2 px perimeter, 44 px targets), laws-of-ux (proximity 8–12 in group / 32–48 between groups, von Restorff reserved for the needs-you card, Hick capped at 3–5 primary options per screen), animation-discipline (150 ms state confirmation, one-shot only, nothing loops, and under `prefers-reduced-motion` the press and the settle are replaced by an opacity crossfade while the feed keeps running — reduced motion governs animation, not data).

## 9. Signature element, hero thesis, reference lock.

**Signature: the check-in stamp.** Each card carries one round stamp that presses once — 150 ms, `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`, a 2 px settle — whenever the live feed re-checks that service, so "checked 2s ago" becoming "12s ago" is a physical mark rather than a text change. The stamp's face carries the state word, so the moment is never colour-only; a new service gets its ON DUTY stamp on publish. Hero thesis: "The board tells you what happened while you were out, who is allowed to act, and never acts for you." Reference lock (mechanics, not palettes): a clinic or school **duty board** where a rotating card per shift is stamped at each check-in, and a library **circulation card** where each visit earns one dated line. A tiny inline-SVG house cat sleeps on the board's edge, sits up when something needs you, and turns to face the door when a service drops off — warmth in the details, never the signal itself, and its posture never replaces the word.

## 10. Risks and UNKNOWNs.

Whether a stamp reads as auditability it cannot deliver is a real risk; it must stay a freshness mark, and the wording says "checked", never "verified". Whether the feed's drifting virtual clock misrepresents real freshness semantics is UNKNOWN. Embed support, sandbox enforcement, the Connect API, disconnect history and long-term retention are UNKNOWN. The cat could drift from decorative into distracting — one posture per state, no idle animation, no blinking loop. Contrast must be measured on implemented pairs before review, and all fixture data stays labelled MOCKUP.

## 11. How it differs from C, and what it keeps.

Kept from C: the real application shell (Home / Connect / Memory / Settings), services as clearly owned labelled places, the owning app framed with its fallback link permanently visible, literal state words, decisions handed off and never made in Worlds, a calm static structure, the MOCKUP label, and the same start state (Media Shelf + Front Door) with Notes Sync as the designed third. Changed: the metaphor is a board of shift cards you read, not a cabinet of fixed bays you select — every card shows its own compact facts inline, so selecting a service reveals depth, not information; the shared fact card is not docked beside the selected bay but is its own Memory surface listing every record with source, freshness, who sees and who acts, which is also what the agent reads; the selected-service `attentionReason`, the first-run board, Settings and the live feed are first-class parts of the design rather than details; and the signature is a check-in stamp on a card rather than an inset bezel docked to a bay. Mechanically, C's dock binds one service's UI to one fact card; this board binds one fact to every view at once, which is the requirement C was already straining toward.

## 12. Desktop and mobile layouts.

**Desktop, 1440px.** Twelve columns: a 200px nav rail (Home · Connect · Memory · Settings, selected item distinct), the board in the remaining main column as three columns of white cards on `#ffffff` with the ring shadow, and a 320px right column carrying the live summary, `#live-toggle`, `#simulate-change` and the needs-you list. Home's header holds the selected service, its state word and `#disconnect`. The service screen is a two-column split: embedded app frame left, that service's facts right. Density is high but rhythm is uneven on purpose — the needs-you card is taller and quieter cards are compact.

**Mobile, 390px.** Designed separately, not squeezed. A bottom tab bar (four destinations, 44 px targets, safe-area padding) replaces the rail; the board becomes one column of full-width cards ordered needs-you → stale → quiet → disconnected history, which is its own reading order rather than the desktop order; each fact row stacks label / value / state-word as three lines at 16px minimum; the feed controls collapse into a single pill row under the Home title with `#live-toggle` and `#simulate-change` side by side; `#add-service` becomes a full-width button pinned at the end of the board; the selected-service header and its `#disconnect` sit directly under the Home title and stay visible at first paint; the service screen is a full-height sheet with a sticky Back control and the Disconnect action in its footer; Connect becomes a full-screen stepper with the step name, a sticky Back / Next bar, and stepping controls as real 44 px buttons. No sideways scroll at 390px: long summaries wrap, timestamps break, tables in Memory become stacked definition rows. Tablet at 768px is a bonus: rail becomes a top bar, board goes two columns, the right column folds under the board.
